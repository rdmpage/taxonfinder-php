# Nomenclatural annotation vocabulary, read by Taxonfinder\Nomenclature.
#
# Three kinds of line, columns separated by spaces or tabs:
#
#   new   <word>                       a word meaning "new"
#   act   <word>  <canonical>  [bare]  a nomenclatural act. Written "bare" it
#                                      may be reported on its own; otherwise it
#                                      only counts next to a "new" word
#   cite  <word>                       a word allowed inside an author citation
#                                      standing between a name and its act
#
# Blank lines and lines starting with # are ignored, and case does not matter.
# A single letter "new" marker is only accepted in lowercase, so that the
# initial in "Amanita sp. N. Smith" is not read as an act.
#
# To add your own without touching this file, put them in
# dictionaries/local/annotations.txt, which is merged on top.

# ---------------------------------------------------------------- new
new   nov
new   nova
new   novum
new   novus
new   n
new   new

# ---------------------------------------------------------------- acts
# Abbreviated. These stand on their own: "Amanita sp." is an indeterminate
# species, not a new one, and is worth reporting as such.
#
# The words spelled out do not. "Genus Tettix, Charp." gives the rank of a
# name in a list and "Key to Cladonotus Species." heads a key; neither is an
# act, and both read as one while these stood alone.
act   sp          sp.        bare
act   spec        sp.        bare
act   species     sp.
act   gen         gen.       bare
act   genus       gen.
act   comb        comb.      bare
# Not standing alone: 'Nom.' abbreviates Nomenclator in botanical citations -
# 'Bauhinia cheilantha (Bongard) Steudel, Nom. Bot.' - and a bare nom.
# announces nothing anyway. The real ones (nom. nud., nom. illeg., nom. cons.)
# are not acts either, and nom. nov. goes by the "new" word.
act   nom         nom.
act   subsp       subsp.     bare
act   ssp         subsp.     bare
act   var         var.       bare
act   fam         fam.       bare
act   subgen      subgen.    bare
act   subfam      subfam.    bare
act   forma       forma      bare

# 'form.' is the abbreviation of forma, but 'form' is also an everyday word -
# 'the form of the pronotum' - so unlike forma it does not stand on its own
# and only counts next to a "new" word, the way 'f' does.
act   form        forma
act   ined        ined.      bare
act   emend       emend.     bare

# Single letters. A bare "f." after a name is as likely to be a figure or a
# female symbol as a forma, and a bare "g." is anyone's guess, so these only
# count next to a "new" word - "GREENIDEA, n. g.", "Hyalopterus, g. n."
act   f           f.
act   g           gen.

# Spelled out, as in "Merragata quieta Drake, new species" and
# "M. hebroides B.-White (new synonymy)". These are ordinary English words, so
# none of them stands alone - "a synonym of harveyi" is not an act.
act   combination   comb.
act   combinations  comb.
act   name          nom.
act   names         nom.
act   subspecies    subsp.
act   variety       var.
act   varieties     var.
act   family        fam.
act   genera        gen.

# ---------------------------------------------------------------- judgments
# A view taken of names already published, rather than the publication of one.
# 'syn. nov.' sinks a name into another and 'stat. nov.' moves one to another
# rank; both speak of names established elsewhere, by somebody else, and
# neither puts a new name into the world. Reported under nomenclature
# .judgments, apart from .acts.
#
# Read exactly as an act is, so 'syn. n.' and 'NEW SYNONYMY' both work.

judgment  syn           syn.       bare
judgment  synonym       syn.
judgment  synonyms      syn.
judgment  synonymy      syn.
judgment  synonymies    syn.
judgment  stat          stat.      bare
judgment  status        stat.

# The standing of a name under the Code. None of these publishes anything -
# they say a name already published is not available, or is illegitimate, or
# is to be kept or set aside. Terms follow the GBIF nomenclatural status
# vocabulary, <https://rs.gbif.org/vocabulary/gbif/nomenclatural_status.xml>.
#
# 'nom. nov.' is not here: a replacement name really is published, so it stays
# an act. And 'dub' and 'conf' are left alone, being already spoken for by
# species dubia and confer.

judgment  nud           nom. nud.       bare
judgment  nudum         nom. nud.       bare
judgment  subnud        nom. subnud.    bare
judgment  subnudum      nom. subnud.    bare
judgment  illeg         nom. illeg.     bare
judgment  illegit       nom. illeg.     bare
judgment  illegitimum   nom. illeg.     bare
judgment  inval         nom. inval.     bare
judgment  invalidum     nom. inval.     bare
judgment  cons          nom. cons.      bare
judgment  conservandum  nom. cons.      bare
judgment  rej           nom. rej.       bare
judgment  rejic         nom. rej.       bare
judgment  rejiciendum   nom. rej.       bare
judgment  superfl       nom. superfl.   bare
judgment  superfluum    nom. superfl.   bare
judgment  oblit         nom. oblit.     bare
judgment  oblitum       nom. oblit.     bare
judgment  protect       nom. protect.   bare
judgment  protectum     nom. protect.   bare
judgment  provis        nom. provis.    bare
judgment  provisorium   nom. provis.    bare
# 'orth. var.' is left out: 'var' is an act in its own right, so the phrase
# would be read twice, once whole and once as a stray 'var.'

# ---------------------------------------------------------------- qualifiers
# Open nomenclature: how sure the identification is, not that anything is new.
# Sigovini, Keppel & Tagliapietra 2016, Open Nomenclature in the biodiversity
# era, Methods Ecol Evol 7:1217-1225, and its Appendix S1.
#
#   qualifier  <word>  <canonical>
#
# These never take a "new" word. A qualifier is reported under nomenclature
# .qualifiers, apart from .acts, because the two say different things: an act
# publishes a name, a qualifier declines to.

qualifier  cfr          cf.
qualifier  conf         cf.
qualifier  aff          aff.

# ---------------------------------------------------------------- joining
# Words that may stand between two acts, as in 'gen. et sp. nov.'. They only
# count where an act really does follow, so 'sp. nov. and Felis leo' still
# ends at the name.

join  and

# ones that also belong to a citation rather than to ordinary prose.
